How to play

Using things: everything works with the keyboard or the mouse. Walk up to something, face it and press E, or simply click it and your character walks there. With the watering can or seeds in hand, face the soil (or click it) and press E to water or plant. Devotees, animals, trees, flowers, statues, lamps and furniture can all be used.

Garden: the temple flower garden is on the left of the temple, looked after by Subala. The garden shed keeps the watering can, seed bags and baskets for flowers and fruit. Take seeds to the brown soil, water them, and pick them when they bloom. Bring 5 flowers to Vrinda for a garland.

Shoes: take your shoes off at the shoe rack beside the temple door before going in, and put them back on when you leave.

Temple: walk in through the big doors. Take the ghee lamp from its stand or incense from the puja tray and offer it at the altar. Pick fruits from the mango and banana trees and offer them too. Offered fruits become prasadam at Chitra's prasadam stall.

Be the pujari: bathe and put on fresh clothes in the ashram (the building next to the goshala), wash your hands in the pujari room, then ask Pujari Damodara if you may offer the arati. Press E for each step.

Pujari room: the curtain on the right of the temple room leads to the pujari's wash basin and the Deity kitchen. The prasadam kitchen and hall are in the green-roofed building by Chitra's prasadam stall.

Services: take the broom, mop or dusting cloth from the cleaning closet in the temple, pick Tulasi leaves for Krishna's feet, get milk from Sridama and cook sweet rice in the kitchen, fan the Deities with the chamara, blow the conch, light the evening lamps on the path, and share prasadam with friends.

Festivals: open the Festivals list at the top and press Celebrate. Radha-Syamasundara are dressed in the festival colours, devotees gather in the temple for a class, then there may be an abhisheka (bathing ceremony), arati with kirtan, and a feast at the prasadam hall. Some festivals have their own special event: Ratha Yatra, the boat festival, the swing, lamps or a hill of food.

Govardhana: walk up to the little cave in Govardhana Hill to visit Giriraja and see the story.

Friends: talk to any devotee to chat, sing kirtan together, or go on a japa walk. On a japa walk your friend follows you, chants with you and bows when you bow.
